# HybridBrainViz — Multi-Region Segmentation and Visualization Architecture

Document ID: 02.9A
Version: 1.0
Status: Architecture Specification
Parent Document: 02.9_Visualization_Algorithms_and_Evaluation_Framework.md
System: HybridBrainViz
Research Context: Hybrid Visualization Framework for Multi-Modal Brain Tumor

# 1. Purpose

This document specifies the architecture for representing, processing, rendering, and evaluating multiple clinically meaningful tumor regions within HybridBrainViz.

The architecture extends the general segmentation-to-visualization pipeline by explicitly supporting:

- multi-modal MRI;
- AI-generated 3D segmentation;
- tumor-region derivation;
- Whole Tumor (WT);
- Tumor Core (TC);
- Enhancing Tumor (ET);
- region-specific boundaries;
- region-specific distance fields;
- region-specific boundary confidence;
- region-aware surface generation;
- region-aware volume rendering;
- region-aware hybrid visualization;
- region-aware boundary enhancement;
- region-aware evaluation.

The objective is not to develop a new tumor-classification algorithm. Instead, HybridBrainViz treats the segmentation produced by an upstream segmentation system such as 3D BEFUnet as structured spatial information that can be represented and enhanced through the visualization pipeline.

# 2. Research Motivation

A conventional visualization pipeline may reduce the segmentation output to a single binary representation:

flowchart TD
    MRI["MRI"]
    SEG["Segmentation"]
    TUMOR["Tumor"]
    SV["Surface / Volume"]

    MRI --> SEG
    SEG --> TUMOR
    TUMOR --> SV

Such a representation loses important internal structure.

For brain-tumor visualization, the system should preserve the distinction between different tumor regions and their spatial relationships.

The proposed architecture therefore adopts:

flowchart TD
    MRI["Multi-Modal MRI"]
    SEG["3D Segmentation"]
    TR["Tumor Region Representation"]
    REG["WT / TC / ET"]
    RV["Region-Aware Visualization"]
    RBE["Region-Aware Boundary Enhancement"]
    HV["Hybrid Visualization"]

    MRI --> SEG
    SEG --> TR
    TR --> REG
    REG --> RV
    RV --> RBE
    RBE --> HV

This allows HybridBrainViz to visualize not merely where a tumor exists, but how its constituent regions are spatially organized.

3. Terminology

A critical terminology distinction is required.

# 3.1 Tumor Types

Tumor types refer to diagnostic or pathological categories, such as particular tumor entities or histological diagnoses.

HybridBrainViz does not claim to determine these diagnoses.

# 3.2 Tumor Regions

WT, TC, and ET are treated within this architecture as tumor regions/subregions derived from the segmentation representation.

Whole Tumor — WT

Represents the complete tumor extent according to the segmentation definition.

Tumor Core — TC

Represents the tumor-core region derived from the segmentation.

Enhancing Tumor — ET

Represents the enhancing tumor component.

Therefore, the visualization architecture deals with:

$$
\text{Tumor Regions} \neq \text{Tumor Types}
$$

This distinction must remain consistent throughout the software, thesis, experiments, and publications.

# 5. Design Objectives

The subsystem shall satisfy the following objectives.

O1 — Preserve segmentation semantics

The visualization must retain the semantic identity of each supported tumor region.

O2 — Preserve spatial relationships

The system must maintain the spatial relationship between WT, TC, and ET.

O3 — Support multiple visualization representations

A region must be renderable as:

- volume;
- surface;
- hybrid representation;
- boundary enhancement;
- annotation.

# O4 — Support independent visibility

Users should be able to independently:

- enable;
- disable;
- hide;
- isolate;
- emphasize

individual regions.

# O5 — Support region-specific rendering parameters

Each region may have independent:

- opacity;
- color;
- transfer-function parameters;
- boundary strength;
- surface visibility;
- rendering mode.
# O6 — Remain renderer-independent

The domain representation must not depend on:

- OpenGL;
- Vulkan;
- DirectX;
- GLSL;
- HLSL.

Those belong to the graphics abstraction layer.

# O7 — Support future tumor regions

The architecture must not hard-code the entire visualization pipeline around only WT, TC, and ET.

Future datasets or research extensions may introduce additional semantic regions.

$$

# 15. Region Boundary Extraction

For every region R, define a boundary:

$$
B_R = \partial R
$$

Therefore:

$$
B_{WT} = \partial WT
$$

$$
B_{TC} = \partial TC
$$

$$
B_{ET} = \partial ET
$$

Where $B_R$ denotes the boundary of region $R$, while $B_{WT}$, $B_{TC}$, and $B_{ET}$ denote the boundaries of the Whole Tumor (WT), Tumor Core (TC), and Enhancing Tumor (ET), respectively.

The architecture therefore produces:

flowchart TD
    WT["WT"] --> BWT["B_WT"]
    TC["TC"] --> BTC["B_TC"]
    ET["ET"] --> BET["B_ET"]

These boundary representations are independent domain data.

16. Boundary Representation

A boundary can be represented as:

- voxel boundary mask;
- signed distance field;
- unsigned distance field;
- extracted surface;
- GPU texture representation.

The domain should not dictate the storage implementation.

Therefore:

flowchart TD
    IBR["IBoundaryRepresentation"]

    IBR --> BM["BoundaryMask"]
    IBR --> DF["DistanceField"]
    IBR --> SB["SurfaceBoundary"]
    IBR --> GBV["GPUBoundaryVolume"]

This preserves implementation flexibility.

# 17. Distance Field

For each region:

$$
D_R(x) = d(x, B_R)
$$

where d represents spatial distance from point x to the region boundary.

The distance must ultimately support physical units where possible.

If voxel spacing is:

$$
(s_x, s_y, s_z)
$$

distance calculations should account for anisotropic spacing rather than assuming:

$$
s_x = s_y = s_z
$$

This is important for medical-volume correctness.

Where:

- $D_R(x)$ — distance from voxel $x$ to the boundary of region $R$.
- $B_R$ — boundary representation of region $R$.
- $(s_x, s_y, s_z)$ — voxel spacing along the three spatial axes.
- $s_x = s_y = s_z$ — isotropic voxel spacing.
- $C_R(x)$ — confidence value associated with region $R$ at position $x$.

# 18. Region Boundary Confidence

For each region we derive:

$$
C_R(x)
$$

from the boundary distance and other relevant signals.

A possible initial formulation is:

$$
C_R(x) = \exp\left(-\frac{D_R(x)^2}{2\sigma_R^2}\right)
$$

This produces:

flowchart TD
    B["Boundary"]
    HC["High Confidence"]
    D["Confidence Decays"]
    IE["Interior / Exterior"]

    B --> HC
    HC --> D
    D --> IE

This illustrates the spatial decay of boundary confidence away from the tumor boundary. The exact function remains an implementation parameter rather than a fixed architectural requirement.

# 19. Multi-Region Boundary Fusion
Because multiple region boundaries can coexist, the system needs a fusion stage.

flowchart TD
    CWT["C_WT"]
    CTC["C_TC"]
    CET["C_ET"]
    F["Boundary Fusion"]
    U["Unified Boundary Field"]

    CWT --> F
    CTC --> F
    CET --> F
    F --> U

A simple formulation is:

$$
C(x) = \max_{R \in \{WT,TC,ET\}} \left(w_R C_R(x)\right)
$$

where: 

$$
R \in \{WT, TC, ET\}
$$

An alternative is weighted accumulation:

$$
C(x) = \sum_{R \in \{WT,TC,ET\}} w_R C_R(x)
$$

Where:

- $C_R(x)$ is the confidence field for region $R$.
- $w_R$ is the weight assigned to region $R$.
- $C(x)$ is the resulting unified boundary confidence field.
- $R \in {WT, TC, ET}$ represents the three tumor regions.
The implementation should support both strategies.

# 22. Region-Aware Volume Rendering

The volume renderer receives:

MRI Volume
+
Segmentation/Region Volume

Conceptually:

$$
V(x) = \{I_1(x), I_2(x), \ldots, I_n(x), L(x)\}
$$

where:
$$ I_i $$ = MRI modality;
L = semantic region label.

The transfer-function system can then evaluate:

$$
TF(I,L)
$$

instead of simply:

$$
TF(I)
$$

This allows segmentation-aware DVR.

Where:

- $V(x)$ — multi-modal voxel representation at position $x$.
- $I_1(x), \ldots, I_n(x)$ — intensity values from the available MRI modalities.
- $L(x)$ — segmentation label at position $x$.
- $TF(I,L)$ — label-aware transfer function, incorporating both intensity and segmentation information.
- $TF(I)$ — conventional intensity-based transfer function.

# 23. Region-Aware Transfer Functions

The transfer-function architecture becomes:

flowchart TD
    TF["TransferFunction"]

    TF --> IF["IntensityFunction"]
    TF --> RF["RegionFunction"]
    TF --> OF["OpacityFunction"]
    TF --> CF["ColorFunction"]

Conceptually:

$$

This expresses the proposed context-aware transfer function, where the rendered appearance depends not only on MRI intensity $I$, but also on the tumor region and estimated boundary confidence.

Therefore:

# 29. Multi-Modal Boundary Enhancement

Boundary enhancement should not necessarily depend only on the segmentation.

A stronger formulation uses multiple signals:

$$
C_B = f\left(G_{MRI}, B_{seg}, D_{boundary}, P_{surface}, I_{multi\text{-}modal}\right)
$$

Where:

$C_B$ — boundary confidence.
$G_{MRI}$ — MRI gradient information.
$B_{seg}$ — segmentation-derived boundary field.
$D_{boundary}$ — distance to the segmentation boundary.
$P_{surface}$ — proximity to the rendered tumor surface.
$I_{multi\text{-}modal}$ — multi-modal MRI information.

This defines boundary confidence as a fusion of image-derived, segmentation-derived, geometric, and multi-modal evidence.

              Multi-modal MRI
                    │
             ┌──────┴──────┐
             ▼             ▼
        MRI Gradient   Intensity
             │             │
             └──────┬──────┘
                    │
Segmentation ──► Boundary
                    │
                    ▼
             Distance Field
                    │
Surface ─────► Surface Proximity
                    │
                    ▼
             Boundary Confidence
                    │
                    ▼
          Adaptive Enhancement

This is where the actual research contribution can become more sophisticated than simply drawing a colored outline around the segmentation.

# 30. Region-Specific Gradient Analysis

For each region R, the system may evaluate local image gradients:

$$
G_R(x) = \left\|\nabla I(x)\right\|
$$

The gradient provides information about intensity transitions.

Combining it with segmentation-derived boundary information allows the system to distinguish:

flowchart TD
    EDGE["Strong MRI Edge"]
    BOUND["Segmentation Boundary"]
    SURF["Near Tumor Surface"]

    CONF["High Boundary Confidence"]

    EDGE --> CONF
    BOUND --> CONF
    SURF --> CONF

from:

flowchart TD
    EDGE["Strong MRI Edge"]
    NOB["No Tumor Boundary"]

    CONF["Lower Tumor-Boundary Confidence"]

    EDGE --> CONF
    NOB --> CONF

This helps reduce indiscriminate edge enhancement.

# 31. Region-Aware Surface Proximity

For a ray-sampled point x, define proximity to a region surface:

$$
P_R(x) = f\left(d(x,S_R)\right)
$$

This allows boundary enhancement to remain spatially associated with the actual tumor geometry.

# 32. Adaptive Enhancement

A general formulation is:

$$
E_R(x) = \alpha_R C_R(x) G_R(x) P_R(x)
$$

Where:

- $P_R(x)$ — surface proximity function for region $R$.
- $d(x,S_R)$ — distance from voxel $x$ to the surface $S_R$.
- $f(\cdot)$ — proximity falloff function.
- $E_R(x)$ — region-specific boundary enhancement strength.
- $\alpha_R$ — enhancement coefficient for region $R$.
- $C_R(x)$ — region boundary confidence.
- $G_R(x)$ — regional MRI gradient magnitude.
- $P_R(x)$ — proximity of $x$ to the corresponding tumor surface.

The actual implementation can later be extended to include additional signals.

The architecture intentionally does not hard-code one mathematical formulation.

# 33. Important: Avoid Overclaiming "Tumor Identification"

The visualization system can support region identification, but we should carefully phrase what it does.

We can claim:

The system enables identification and differentiation of segmented tumor regions such as WT, TC, and ET within a unified 3D visualization.

We should not claim:

The system diagnostically identifies tumor types.

Those are fundamentally different claims.

# 35. View Consistency Also Becomes Region-Aware

For view v:

$$
Q_{R,v}
$$

represents the boundary score of region R from view v.

Then:

$$
\bar{Q}_R = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{v=1}^{N} Q_{R,v}
$$

and:

$$
\sigma_R = \sqrt{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{v=1}^{N}\left(Q_{R,v}-\bar{Q}_R\right)^2}
$$

Where:

- $Q_{R,v}$ — boundary enhancement score for region $R$ at viewing angle $v$.
- $\bar{Q}_R$ — mean view-wise score for region $R$.
- $\sigma_R$ — standard deviation of the region-specific scores across views.
- $N$ — number of evaluated viewing angles.

We can therefore determine whether ET, TC, and WT remain visually distinguishable throughout the camera trajectory.

This directly addresses your earlier concern about relying on manually selected screenshots.
