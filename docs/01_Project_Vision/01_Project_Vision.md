# 1. Introduction

HybridBrainViz is a next-generation research platform dedicated to advancing the visualization of complex medical imaging data through the integration of computer graphics, artificial intelligence, and scientific computing.

The platform is designed to bridge the gap between traditional medical image visualization techniques and modern AI-assisted visualization workflows by providing a modular, extensible, and reproducible software ecosystem.

Unlike conventional visualization software that focuses primarily on image rendering, HybridBrainViz treats visualization as an intelligent process that combines image acquisition, AI segmentation, feature extraction, rendering, quantitative evaluation, and scientific experimentation into a unified framework.

The project serves as both a software engineering initiative and a long-term scientific research platform.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 2. Vision Statement

To become a world-class open research platform for AI-driven medical visualization that enables researchers, clinicians, and software engineers to develop, evaluate, and deploy advanced visualization techniques for medical imaging.

# 3. Mission Statement

The mission of HybridBrainViz is to develop an extensible visualization framework that integrates artificial intelligence, computer graphics, and medical image analysis into a unified ecosystem capable of supporting scientific discovery, clinical research, and future healthcare innovation.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 4. Project Motivation

Medical imaging technologies continue to generate increasingly large, complex, and multi-dimensional datasets.

Modern MRI examinations contain significantly more information than can be effectively interpreted using conventional visualization techniques.

Although AI has dramatically improved medical image segmentation, visualization techniques have not advanced at the same pace.

Current visualization systems often suffer from limitations including:

• Poor representation of tumor boundaries
• Occlusion of clinically relevant structures
• Limited integration between surface and volume rendering
• Weak support for AI-assisted visualization
• Poor reproducibility of visualization experiments
• Difficult extension for future research

HybridBrainViz addresses these limitations by developing an extensible hybrid visualization framework centered on adaptive boundary enhancement and scientific reproducibility.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 5. Research Problem

Existing medical visualization systems generally treat segmentation and rendering as separate processes.

Most visualization pipelines terminate after segmentation, leaving rendering as a largely static post-processing step.

Consequently:

• Boundary information is frequently lost.
• Tumor morphology is not sufficiently emphasized.
• Multi-modal MRI information is underutilized.
• Surface and volume rendering remain disconnected.
• Visualization quality is rarely evaluated quantitatively.

HybridBrainViz aims to redefine visualization as an intelligent, data-driven process.

## Specific Objectives

• Integrate AI-based brain tumor segmentation.
• Support multi-modal MRI visualization.
• Develop hybrid rendering techniques.
• Introduce adaptive boundary enhancement.
• Develop quantitative visualization evaluation methods.
• Enable reproducible visualization experiments.
• Provide an extensible software platform for future research.

# 13. Research Philosophy

Visualization is not merely the act of rendering images.

Visualization is the transformation of complex medical data into clinically meaningful knowledge.

Artificial intelligence should enhance—not replace—human understanding.

Every visualization produced by HybridBrainViz should improve interpretability, reproducibility, and scientific insight.

# 17. Guiding Principle

Every feature added to HybridBrainViz should satisfy three questions:

1. Does it improve scientific understanding?

2. Does it improve software extensibility?

3. Does it improve research reproducibility?

If the answer to any of these questions is "No", the feature should be reconsidered.
